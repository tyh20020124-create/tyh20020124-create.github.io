# Hydroren — Portfolio Narrative Analysis

Status: internal narrative and image plan. This is not yet public webpage copy.

## Core reading of the project

Hydroren is a concept and prototype for a robotic cleaner designed around the daily rhythm of a commercial swimming pool. It cleans while the pool is closed, supports routine maintenance by pool staff, and later turns cleaning records into visible information for swimmers.

The project brings together three ideas that should remain connected throughout the webpage. A single suction motor produces the water flow used for cleaning, while that same flow is redirected to support brushing and movement. A hydrocyclone separates suspended particles to reduce dependence on a dense filter. A circular display gives staff operational feedback and presents recent cleaning information to swimmers when the robot is docked.

The strongest portfolio story is therefore not simply a new pool-cleaner form. It is a robotic service system that links resource use, maintenance work, and public trust.

**Design principle**  
Use one physical process to do more, then make its results understandable.

## Why this framing works

The presentation contains several possible stories: energy use, hydrocyclone separation, product form, autonomous navigation, pool management, and water-quality communication. Giving each topic equal weight would make the webpage feel like a compressed graduation presentation.

The better structure treats them as one chain. Commercial pools need regular cleaning, but the process remains largely invisible to swimmers and can demand energy, water, and repeated filter maintenance. Hydroren reorganises that process around a shared hydraulic system. The resulting cleaning record then becomes a communication layer between the pool manager and the public.

This turns the project from a collection of features into a clear design argument:

`Invisible maintenance → resource-aware cleaning system → overnight operation → visible cleaning record`

## Recommended page narrative

### Opening — Cleaning the pool is only half of the problem

The page should open with the underwater scenario render and a short introduction. Commercial pools depend on cleaning routines that swimmers rarely see. Even when staff maintain the water carefully, the absence of visible information can leave people uncertain about cleanliness. Existing robotic cleaners also separate suction, movement, brushing, and filtration into systems that increase energy use and maintenance work.

The opening should establish a double challenge: improve how the machine cleans and improve how people understand that cleaning has happened.

Do not lead with market-size charts or unsupported health statistics. Those helped structure the student project, but they distract from the more credible and distinctive design problem.

### System idea — One water flow, several jobs

The second section should explain the core hydraulic idea. Hydroren uses a primary motor to create suction. Instead of treating the resulting flow only as an exhaust, the design redirects it to rotate brushes and support propulsion. The project asks whether the energy already moving water through the machine can also perform secondary mechanical work.

This section needs a simple custom diagram for the final website:

`motor and impeller → suction and particle intake → separated flow → brushing and directional movement`

The existing “Solutions” slide contains the right logic, but its three-card layout should not be pasted directly into the webpage. The mechanism should be redrawn in the portfolio’s visual language and presented as a single continuous system.

### Separation — Reducing the burden of the filter

The third section should focus on the hydrocyclone. Tangential flow creates a vortex that moves denser particles toward the collection chamber while cleaner water returns to the circulation path. This provides a plausible route to collecting debris before it reaches a fine filter, reducing how often staff need to remove and rinse that filter.

The webpage should describe this as a design investigation supported by simulation and a physical test rig. It should not state the presentation’s efficiency figures as final product performance unless the original experiment records can confirm the exact method and result.

The best image pair is the cyclone-flow simulation next to the transparent water-tank test. Together they show both the proposed mechanism and the attempt to test it physically.

### Form development — A low body shaped for coverage and flow

The development section should compress the many sketches into one visual progression. Early concepts moved from conventional rectangular cleaners toward a low triangular body with three extended cleaning points. The geometry gives the machine a stable footprint, keeps the main volume close to the pool floor, and creates three leading edges that can approach walls and corners.

The sketches also show how the external form grew around the internal flow path, central separator, intake, brushes, and directional nozzles. This is more important than presenting the form as biomimicry alone. The ray-like appearance is a useful visual association, but the website should emphasise how the body mediates between the pool surface, the hydraulic layout, and handling by staff.

Use one wide process image followed by the final annotated sketch. Avoid showing every draft model at equal size.

### Service rhythm — Working at night, communicating by day

The user journey gives Hydroren a distinctive temporal structure. A manager places the robot in the pool after closing. The machine cleans overnight, collects debris, and records its operation. In the morning, staff remove the collection chamber, place the robot on its wall-mounted charging base, and switch the interface from operational feedback to a public display.

This section should combine the manager and swimmer perspectives into one concise timeline rather than reproducing both dense journey slides. The key transition is the dock: it acts as a charging point, storage position, and public information surface.

Recommended sequence:

1. Deploy after closing
2. Clean and collect overnight
3. Empty the collection chamber
4. Dock and recharge
5. Display recent cleaning information during opening hours

### Interface — Two levels of information on one circular display

The circular interface supports two audiences. During operation, staff need immediate status such as cleaning progress, battery level, and mode. While docked, swimmers need a calmer summary of recent cleaning time and collected material rather than detailed machine controls.

The webpage should show no more than three interface states: work progress, charging, and the public cleaning summary. The current UI assets are useful evidence of the interaction concept, but their labels and metrics should be edited before publication so they communicate only information the prototype actually measured.

### Prototype and engineering — Testing the system at several scales

The final section should bring together the 80% appearance model, the smaller docked model, the hydrocyclone test rig, and one restrained technical drawing. This demonstrates that the project moved across form, interaction, and mechanism rather than ending as a render.

The engineering drawings can support the story, but a full specification table would create false product-level precision. Use the exploded view or cross-section to explain the internal arrangement. Treat dimensions, battery capacity, runtime, sensing method, and performance values as design targets unless they were measured on an integrated working prototype.

### Closing — Maintenance as part of the public experience

Hydroren proposes that sustainable product design can involve both how a machine uses resources and how its work becomes legible. The project turns pool cleaning from a hidden back-of-house task into a visible service: the robot works when the pool is empty, then communicates a simple record of that work when people return.

The wall-mounted scenario is an effective closing image because it shows the product between its two roles: an underwater robot at night and a public-facing object during the day.

## Claims that need caution before publication

The presentation includes market projections, survey percentages, health figures, energy comparisons, separation efficiency, filter-replacement reduction, runtime, and other engineering specifications. Several may come from student research or design calculations rather than controlled validation. They should not appear as definitive public claims until their sources and methods are checked.

For the first webpage version, the narrative can safely say that the project explored energy reuse, hydrocyclone separation, reduced filter maintenance, overnight autonomous cleaning, and visible cleaning records. Quantitative claims can be added later only where the underlying evidence is available.

## Draft short-form description

Hydroren is a robotic cleaner designed for the daily rhythm of a commercial swimming pool. It explores how the water flow created for suction can also support brushing and movement, while a hydrocyclone separates collected particles before they reach a fine filter.

The robot works overnight, when the pool is closed. In the morning, staff empty the collection chamber and place it on a wall-mounted charging base. Its circular display then changes from operational feedback to a simple record of recent cleaning, making maintenance more visible to swimmers.

Developed through hydraulic simulation, physical separation tests, form studies, interface design, and scale prototypes, Hydroren treats pool cleaning as both a robotic task and a service people should be able to understand.
